What the graph is teaching

A logistic model connects three different scales.

1
Linear predictor

The model adds weighted evidence: η = β₀ + β₁(attendance) + β₂(prior achievement).

2
Log-odds → odds

Exponentiating η gives odds. An odds ratio above 1 means higher odds; below 1 means lower odds.

3
Odds → probability

The inverse-logit compresses any log-odds value into a probability between 0 and 1.

4
Interpret cautiously

A prediction is not a causal effect. Attendance may reflect many other supports and circumstances.

Education-research grounding

The scenario echoes education early-warning research that examines attendance, course performance, and prior achievement as indicators associated with later educational outcomes. The coefficients in this lab are intentionally simulated for learning; they are not copied from a particular study.

Suggested readings: Allensworth, E. M., & Easton, J. Q. (2007). What matters for staying on-track and graduating in Chicago Public High Schools. Consortium on Chicago School Research. Also see: Raju, D., & Schumacker, R. (2015). Exploring student characteristics of retention using logistic regression. International Journal of Higher Education, 4(4), 112–123.

Instructor note: Invite students to distinguish prediction from explanation, identify the unit of a coefficient, and discuss what omitted variables could make an early-warning model unfair or misleading.
